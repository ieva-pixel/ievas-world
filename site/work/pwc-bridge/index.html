PwC Bridge

Designing a custom workspace that gave PwC Switzerland's teams everything they needed in one easy-to-use place.

Role Lead UX/UI Designer
Client PwC Switzerland
Scope Research, IA, UX, UI, handoff

Context

From idea to interface, my first UX leadership role.

In January 2024, I joined Project Bridge to bring scattered tools and information into one platform, tailored to each person’s role.

The aim was to make everyday work easier. Teams needed one place to access client data, manage engagements, check risk status, start engagement letters and open the tools they used most.

My focus was to make these tasks clear and straightforward, with a consistent UI that could grow with the platform. We tested the designs with teams and refined them based on how people used Bridge in their daily work.

My role

Lead and sole UX/UI designer.

I was part of a team of around ten, working closely with two developers and a project manager, with regular feedback from stakeholders across the business.

We worked within strict brand and design constraints, using PwC’s existing component library as our starting point. Building a new tool meant finding new ways to use those components, combining them or creating new ones when existing options did not fit.

Any additions or changes had to go through two rounds of approval: first the central design team, then the local brand team. This made the process lengthy and required careful planning.

Although the approval process took time, the work had a wider impact. Components I created for Bridge are now used by teams across PwC globally.

Stakeholder dynamics

Stakeholder expectations did not always match everyday user needs.

The PwC partners funding Bridge were also users, but they did not rely on it daily. Their requirements carried significant weight, even when feedback from frequent users pointed in a different direction. This limited some design decisions and meant making compromises.

Explaining design decisions became a key part of my role.

I used findings from user research to explain what people needed and why certain design choices mattered, whether I was speaking to partners, developers or tax specialists.

The pilot launched eleven months after kickoff.

Despite the complexity of the environment and the constraints of a locked design system, the pilot version went live in November 2024, less than a year after I joined.

Outcomes

Bridge is now widely used across PwC Switzerland, with plans to expand internationally.

Launching the platform was just the start. Feedback from users helped us identify confusing steps, improve existing features and decide what to build next.

For me, the biggest lesson was that good design takes research, patience and persistence. Working within strict constraints pushed me to find practical, well-designed solutions and explain why they mattered.

11 mo From kickoff to pilot

Learnings

What this project taught me about design leadership.

01

I learnt when to push and when to adapt.

Some design decisions were worth challenging; others needed a practical compromise. Working on Bridge helped me make those calls and explain my reasoning.

02

I had to make everyday users’ needs heard.

Partners funded the project and shaped its direction, but they used Bridge less often than our daily users. I learnt to present research clearly when their expectations pointed us towards different solutions.

03

The pilot helped us see what needed to change.

After eleven months, we had a working platform that teams could use. Their feedback gave us a clearer view of what worked, what caused confusion and what was missing.

04

Patience helped good ideas go further.

New components needed approval from both the central design team and the local brand team. The process took time, but some of my work now supports teams across PwC globally.
