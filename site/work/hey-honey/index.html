Vision

Make local honey easy to find, fair to buy, and worth subscribing to.

Hey Honey is a mobile-first marketplace that connects consumers with local beekeepers, making it easy to discover and buy high-quality honey, and giving farmers a fair, digital-first sales channel.

Users can filter by honey type, subscribe to regular deliveries, purchase gifts, and explore weekly recipes. Farmers benefit from increased visibility and a transparent route to market, without depending on supermarket shelf space.

Competitor audit

Strong storytelling, weak product features.

Bee Farm

Easy to subscribe, harder to make changes.

The site clearly explains where the honey comes from and shows its certifications. Subscribing is straightforward, but pausing or skipping a delivery is difficult. Filters and customer reviews are also limited.

Honig.ch

A personal story, but little product information.

The family business shares the story behind its honey, but individual products need more detail. The site is only available in German and has no reviews, filters or subscription option.

Takeaway

What this meant for Hey Honey?

Show who makes the honey and where it comes from. Help buyers compare products, read reviews and manage deliveries easily.

User research

Four weeks of user research.

I carried out four in-depth interviews to understand people’s habits and reasons for buying honey, followed by a survey with ten people to explore the patterns further. I grouped the findings into six themes to guide the design.

What I wanted to find out

Where do people buy honey? What makes them choose one over another? Would they buy it as a gift or subscribe to regular deliveries? Five interview questions and seven survey questions helped me explore these topics.

Local honey needed to be easier to find.

Many participants wanted to buy local honey, but usually chose the supermarket because it was convenient. Making local options easier to access would give them more choice based on price, flavour and where the honey was produced.

People valued local honey and also bought it as a gift.

Participants associated local honey with better quality, but price and availability made it harder to buy. Gifting also came up in the research, suggesting it was worth making this option easy to find and use.

People were open to subscribing, with a few conditions.

Seven out of ten respondents were open to regular deliveries, but wanted clear terms and the freedom to make changes. The research helped me focus on four things: making honey easier to find, keeping subscriptions flexible, explaining the details clearly and making gifting straightforward.

Personas

Three personas to guide the design.

Persona 01

Everyday Ella

Buying honey is part of Ella’s usual supermarket shop. She is interested in local options, but price and convenience matter when choosing where to buy.

Persona 02

Conscious Luca

Luca wants to know where the honey comes from and who produces it. He would consider a subscription if he could easily change or pause deliveries.

Persona 03

Gift Isabell

Isabell buys honey for special occasions. She wants attractive packaging, a personal message and delivery straight to the person receiving it.

Task flows

Three journeys, one for each persona, mobile-first.

I created journey maps for one-off purchases, subscriptions and gifts, covering each step from finding honey to placing an order. I used them to consider how buyers might feel along the way and where they could get stuck.

Sign-in is offered before checkout, alongside a guest option for first-time buyers. I kept mobile steps short, using expandable details and pop-up windows. Desktop layouts include side filters, detailed beekeeper profiles and checkout on a single page.

MVP scope

A simple, trustworthy first launch.

  • Must haveFilterable catalogue, product detail, cart, checkout, "Subscribe & Save" with flexible management, ratings & reviews, farmer profiles.
  • Should haveGifting, weekly recipes, order tracking, account & preferences.
  • Could haveLoyalty programme, advanced personalisation, taste-led recommendations.
  • Won't have (yet)Marketplace seller dashboard, B2B wholesale, multi-region shipping.

For the first version, I prioritised buying honey and setting up a subscription. Reviews, ratings and beekeeper profiles would help buyers understand what they were buying and who they were buying from.

Gifting, recipes and order tracking were useful additions, but could wait if needed. Loyalty programmes and more personalised recommendations were planned for later, once we could learn from how people used the platform.

Information architecture

Keeping pages within easy reach.

The site has five main sections: Shop, Subscriptions, Gifts, Profile and About, with no more than three levels of navigation. Help and FAQs are available in the footer.

Mobile navigation focuses on the main tasks through a top tab bar. On desktop, dropdown menus and links between related pages give buyers more ways to find honey.
