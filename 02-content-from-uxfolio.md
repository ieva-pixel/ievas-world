## Project 01 — UX/UI Process for Hey Honey

**Tagline:** A curated marketplace for high-quality, locally produced honey

### Project Vision
Hey Honey is a mobile-first marketplace that connects consumers with local beekeepers, making it easy to discover and buy high-quality, locally produced honey. Users can filter by honey type, subscribe to regular deliveries, purchase gifts, and explore weekly recipes. Farmers benefit from a fair, digital-first sales channel with increased visibility.

### Project Goals
- Simplify honey discovery and purchase
- Build trust through reviews and transparent sourcing
- Drive recurring revenue via subscriptions
- Support local farmers
- Inspire customers with recipe content

### Success Metrics (KPIs)
- Conversion rate ≥ 5%
- Subscription adoption ≥ 20%
- Gift purchase share ≥ 15%
- Review activity ≥ 30%
- Farmer adoption ≥ 50 in year one

### Scope (MVP)
- Filterable honey catalog & product detail pages
- Cart, checkout, gift & subscription options
- Ratings, reviews, and recipe section
- Farmer profiles

### Competitor Audit (UX/UI)
**Bee Farm** — Small artisan producer with a few products and a subscription. Strong storytelling, clear provenance and certifications, simple subscription flow, clean catalogue. Weak on filters, reviews, and rigid subscription options. Takeaway: good storytelling and simple subscription, but needs more flexible features and feedback.

**Honig.ch** — Family-run shop selling Swiss honey and rarities, story-driven site. Authentic artisan story, simple product presentation with clear prices. Missing product descriptions, no reviews, no filters or subscription, German-only. Takeaway: great provenance storytelling, but limited e-commerce features.

### User Research (qualitative + quantitative)
Conducted four in-depth interviews and a 10-person survey to explore how people choose honey, where they buy it, and what would motivate them to try local or subscription options.

Most participants buy honey in supermarkets for convenience, even though many would prefer local products if they were easier to access. Quality and authenticity matter — users trust local or organic sources but find them harder to find or more expensive. Price and flavor are key decision factors; packaging or branding play a smaller role.

Honey is also perceived as a warm, thoughtful gift — several participants have gifted it for birthdays or get-well occasions. Subscriptions sparked interest mainly when flexible — users want control to pause, skip, or cancel anytime, and appreciate reward systems or small perks.

### Personas
- **Everyday Ella** — Values convenience and affordability; buys honey in supermarkets but would prefer local options if easier to access.
- **Conscious Luca** — Seeks authentic, local honey and wants transparency about origin and quality; open to subscriptions if flexible.
- **Gift Isabell** — Enjoys giving meaningful, local gifts; looks for elegant packaging and personalisation options.

### User Journeys & Task Flows
Mapped three key journeys: product purchase, subscription setup, and gift purchase. Each visualised step by step — from discovery to checkout — to identify emotional peaks, drop-offs, and improvement opportunities. Login/register added before checkout to support order tracking and personalisation, while still allowing guest checkout.

Mobile flows optimised for speed and minimal steps (progressive disclosure, inline modals); desktop flows provide more context (side filters, one-page checkout, detailed farmer info).

### Feature Prioritisation (MoSCoW)
MVP focuses on a simple, trustworthy shopping and subscription experience — product discovery with filters, product details, checkout, and a basic "Subscribe & Save" flow with flexible management. Reviews, ratings, and farmer profiles support transparency and trust. Gifting, recipes, and order tracking marked "Should Have"; loyalty programs and advanced personalisation planned for future.

### Information Architecture
Clear, intuitive IA with no more than three levels of hierarchy. Main nav: Shop, Subscriptions, Gifts, Profile, About. Support and FAQs in footer. Mobile prioritises essential actions via top tab bar; desktop provides broader context with dropdown menus and cross-links.

### Wireframes
Translated IA into low-fidelity screens covering browsing, product selection, subscription, cart, and checkout. Familiar e-commerce patterns adapted for local honey buying — provenance, subscriptions, gift creation. Foundation for interaction design, expressed through a light, pastel, playful UI style reflecting the brand's warm, natural character.

### High-fidelity mockups
The visual direction combines a dark, nature-inspired aesthetic with glassmorphism UI to create a modern, immersive shopping experience — reflecting the warmth, richness, and natural origin of honey while maintaining clarity. Dark background creates strong contrast and lets golden honey illustrations stand out. Honeycomb patterns, dripping honey shapes, and warm amber tones reinforce natural identity. Glassmorphism on cards, search bars, and CTAs — semi-transparent surfaces, soft blurs, subtle gradients. The homepage follows a vertical storytelling structure, gradually introducing the brand, the product, and the people behind it.

## Project 02 — PwC Bridge

**Tagline:** Lead UX/UI Designer | PwC Switzerland | Tax & Technology

> Note: Due to confidentiality agreements with PwC, original platform designs cannot be shared. Visuals in this case study are an independent, conceptual redesign created after the project to illustrate how I would re-imagine the platform without governance, legal, or ownership constraints. Mockups created using AI-assisted tools (Magic Patterns) as part of an exploratory design process.

### From Idea to Interface: My First UX/UI Design Leadership Experience
In January 2024, I was invited to join Project Bridge, an internal PwC Switzerland initiative focused on consolidating fragmented tools and information into a single, role-aware platform. The goal was not visual reinvention but operational clarity: enabling teams to access client data, manage engagements, monitor risk status, initiate engagement letters, and connect frequently used tools from one central workspace.

At the stage I joined and throughout my involvement, UI polish was not the primary focus. The priority was to build a functional, scalable foundation that could be validated in real working conditions and iteratively improved based on actual user behaviour.

### My Role
Lead and sole UX/UI designer on the project, working within a cross-functional team of around 10 people. Closest collaborators: two developers and a project manager, with continuous input from stakeholders across seniority levels. Because the project sat within Tax & Technology rather than a central design team, we operated under strict PwC brand guidelines and an existing component library. While I actively identified areas where components could be adjusted or improved, many suggestions were declined due to governance and ownership constraints. Designing within these limits was a core part of the challenge.

### Stakeholder Dynamics
A unique challenge: many stakeholders were also part of our direct users. Feedback loops were short, expectations high, and decisions often needed to balance user needs, business priorities, and organisational politics. Stakeholder management became as important as interface design.

Despite these complexities, the pilot version was successfully launched in November 2024, less than a year after kickoff.

### Outcomes & Learnings
Following launch, the focus shifted to iterative improvement: analysing user feedback, refining flows, and designing additional features requested by teams actively using the platform. The project strengthened my ability to design pragmatically, make trade-offs, and advocate for user needs within rigid systems.

Project Bridge is now widely used across PwC Switzerland, with plans for expansion into other countries.

More importantly, the project fundamentally shaped my approach to UX/UI design. It reinforced the value of user research in enterprise environments, the reality of designing within strict constraints, and the impact of incremental, evidence-based improvements over visual perfection. It was a clear demonstration that strong UX leadership is not about ideal processes, but about delivering clarity and value in complex, real-world systems.

## Project 03 — Certifaction AG

**Tagline:** Electronic signatures for certification

**Subtitle:** Certifaction AG is the leading provider of secure, privacy-friendly eSignatures

### The 3 Month Challenge

**Month 01** — One initial assignment: conducting a UX analysis on their website and delivering a presentation to the team. Through this task I gained insights into the company itself and the crucial elements to emphasize in such presentations — and the art of providing effective feedback.

**Month 02** — As I gained deeper familiarity with the product, I conducted a comprehensive competitive audit to evaluate how similar platforms addressed key user needs. The analysis uncovered several gaps in our own product offering and highlighted opportunities for improvement. After presenting and discussing findings with the team, we moved forward with new solution concepts. I was responsible for designing a new language selection bar and a plan selection page to better align the product with user expectations and industry standards.

**Month 03** — During brainstorming sessions aimed at improving UX, we identified the need for a structured user onboarding process. The objective: elevate the overall experience, reduce confusion, and boost engagement by guiding first-time users.

Before implementation, usability testing revealed only **3 out of 10 users** were able to complete the core task without asking clarifying questions, with most struggling to understand the initial workflow. After introducing the onboarding flow, follow-up testing showed significant improvement: **7 out of 10 users** completed the same task independently, and reported feeling more confident and oriented within the first minutes of use.

In addition to these responsibilities, I crafted wireframes and mockups, supported the team with UX writing, contributed to branding workshops, assisted marketing with research and case studies, and produced onboarding videos for the web app.

## Project 04 — Share Your Bag

**Tagline:** Revisiting My First UX Project With a Product Mindset

### Project Intro
This project began in 2021 as my very first UX case study. Years later, with significantly more experience and a sharper design perspective, I felt compelled to return to it. Not because it was broken, but because the core idea still felt strong.

Revisiting allowed me to rethink every decision through the lens of what I know now — research depth, usability, accessibility, visual hierarchy. This redesign reflects both my growth as a designer and my belief in the long-term potential of the product. It's a concept I would genuinely love to bring to life one day.

### Overview
The luxury handbag market offers endless variety, yet access to designer bags remains limited to a small audience. Prices have risen dramatically, turning bags into status symbols rather than everyday items. This project explores a peer-to-peer mobile app that lets users rent designer handbags from one another, making luxury more accessible, flexible, and sustainable.

### Problem
Designer handbags are priced far beyond what most people can justify for daily use, yet many consumers are drawn to luxury brands for special occasions or to experience owning a high-end product. At the same time, many designer bags sit unused in closets for most of the year — wasted value and missed opportunity. There is currently no seamless, trust-focused way for individuals to rent these items from each other safely.

### Solution
A peer-to-peer marketplace where users rent and lend designer handbags. Clear pricing, verified users, intuitive booking flow. Makes luxury accessible for short-term use while letting owners monetize items they already own.

The redesign focuses on:
- Building trust through transparency and verification
- Simplifying the rental journey from discovery to return
- Creating a polished, luxury-inspired yet approachable interface
- Incorporating accessibility best practices from the start

### Research & Analysis (2021)
Qualitative interviews with five participants on brand awareness, purchasing habits, spending patterns, and attitudes toward ownership versus access.

**Key Findings:**
- High prices are the primary reason users avoid purchasing designer handbags
- Most participants buy a new bag at least once per season
- Majority prefer shopping online over in-store
- All participants viewed renting designer handbags as a positive alternative to buying
- 4 out of 5 participants would be likely to use a peer-to-peer rental app, either to save money or earn additional income

### Then (2021) → Now
**2021:** First end-to-end UX project. Applied core UX methods across discovery, design, and testing. Usability testing surfaced issues around navigation clarity, pricing visibility, and inconsistent UI patterns — addressed through iterative fixes.

**Now:** Redesigned the experience more intentionally. Updated solution prioritises system consistency, clear affordances, transparent pricing, and stronger brand presence — shifting from screen-level problem solving to a cohesive product experience.

### Key Takeaways
- Consistency and visibility are critical to user confidence and trust
- Strong concepts benefit from revisiting with deeper UX maturity
- Designing systems early prevents downstream usability issues
- User feedback is most effective when paired with clear design principles

### Next Steps
- Validate the product with a broader set of renters and lenders
- Explore trust and safety features (verification, reviews, insurance)
- Test edge cases such as cancellations and damaged items
- Define an MVP scope toward a potential real-world release

**Tools used:** Figma, Material UI, Miro, Mobbin, Lookback, WebAIM, Whimsical, Zeplin
